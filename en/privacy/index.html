PRIVACY

Privacy and cookies.

The short version, without the legalese: here's what happens to your data on this site.

Who I am

This site belongs to Lien Pletinckx, AskLien.ai. If you have a question about your data, email hello@asklien.ai and I'll answer you myself.

Cookies for visitor statistics

I use Google Analytics to see how many people read my pages and which ones are read most. That tells me what to write more about.

This only happens if you give consent on your first visit, either by checking "Statistics" or by clicking "Yes, all of it". If you click "No, thanks", nothing is loaded and nothing is tracked. Your choice is stored on your own device.

What I see: how many visitors, which pages, which channel they came from, and whether they are on a phone or a laptop. What I don't see: who you are. Your IP address is shortened before it is sent, and I never use these statistics for advertising.

Which cookies exactly? Google places _ga and _ga_<code>, which recognize your device so I can tell returning visitors from new ones. They stay for a maximum of two years. I keep the data itself in Google Analytics for fourteen months, after which it is deleted automatically. Google acts as a processor for me, based on the data processing agreement accepted in Google Analytics. Data may be processed on servers outside the European Union, with the European Commission's standard contractual clauses as a safeguard.

Want to change your choice? Click Cookie settings (the link is also at the bottom of every page). If you say no, existing cookies are deleted right away. Your consent expires after six months anyway, and then I ask again.

Cookies for advertising

I advertise on Facebook and Instagram. To know whether those ads work, I use the Meta Pixel. It measures which pages you view here after clicking an ad, and lets me show an ad again later to people who already visited my site.

This also only happens if you give separate consent, either by checking "Advertising" or by clicking "Yes, all of it". If you leave it unchecked, the pixel doesn't load and Meta doesn't know you were here.

Which cookies exactly? Meta places _fbp, which recognizes your browser, and sometimes _fbc, which remembers which ad brought you here. They stay for a maximum of three months.

Meta Platforms Ireland is jointly responsible with me for collecting and forwarding that data. What Meta does with it afterward is covered by Meta's privacy policy. Data may be processed on servers outside the European Union, with the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and the standard contractual clauses as safeguards.

You can withdraw your consent at any time via Cookie settings. The Meta cookies are then deleted right away.

YouTube videos

Some guides contain a YouTube video. I use the privacy-friendly version (youtube-nocookie.com): as long as you don't press play, YouTube places no cookies to follow you. If you do press play, the video loads from YouTube (Google) and their rules apply.

New participants on the site

If you sign up for the Dutch-language challenge, a short notice may appear on the challenge page saying that your first name signed up, and how long ago. Never your last name, email address or city. If you'd rather not, email hello@asklien.ai and I'll take your name out.

The newsletter

If you subscribe, I store your first name and email address with Brevo, the service I use to send my emails. I only use them to send you my newsletter, and I don't sell or share them with anyone. Every email has an unsubscribe link at the bottom, and then you disappear from the list.

Forms and sign-ups

If you fill in a contact form or a survey, it reaches me through FormSubmit. If you pay for the challenge or a membership, that payment goes through Stripe. I see your name and email address there, never your card details.

What you can always ask

You can ask me at any time which data I have about you, have it corrected, or have it deleted completely. Email hello@asklien.ai and I'll take care of it. If you're not satisfied with how I handle it, you can contact the Belgian Data Protection Authority.
