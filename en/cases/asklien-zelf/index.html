01

Why I'm showing this

Every consultant says AI saves time. Very few show you how their own business runs. I think you should be able to check whether someone uses what she sells.

There's a second reason. Everything I build for a client, I built for myself first. That includes the hub where clients follow their project, the status reports and the chatbot monitoring. My own business is where I find out what works before I set it up anywhere else.

02

One project, from inquiry to invoice

Take an AI audit. Here's what happens and where an agent does the work:

Prep. A skill gets everything ready for the audit day: what I already know about the business, the questions I need to ask, and the tools they use.

The report. After the audit day, a second skill turns my notes and the recording into a report in my standard format. I rewrite it, tighten it and decide what stays.

The agreement. A generator drafts the proposal and the agreement in my style, with my terms.

The project. The client gets a project in my hub with phases and tasks. Every meeting comes in automatically, notes included.

Follow-up. A status report is ready every week showing what got done and what's still open, person by person.

The invoice. A skill handles that too.

03

What I still do myself

The audit day. No agent is involved in that. Watching how people work, asking follow-up questions and picking up on where things aren't working is the job itself.

I also read everything before it goes out. A skill writes the first draft, and I decide what gets sent. The weekly email to my members is prepared as a draft every Monday morning, and it doesn't go out until I've read it.

That's my rule for every agent I build, for myself or for a client: the agent does the prep work, and a person makes the call.

Some things need an agent, some need a workflow, and some just need a better system. An audit sorts out which is which.

05

What I've learned

Starting small works better than planning big. My first skill did one thing. I only built the second one after I was using the first every week.

Some things don't need an agent. The payment that creates an account is a plain workflow, and no AI has to think about it. What matters is knowing which one you need when. Figuring that out is what I do in an audit at your business.

06

What I get out of it

I've never tracked my hours, so I won't put a number on it. Here's what I do know:

Staying small while doing a lot.Courses, client projects and a community, run by a small team.
No client falls through the cracks.Every project gets a status update every week, even when I'm traveling.
My style stays consistent.A report or an agreement always looks like it came from me.
My time goes to people.If an agent can prep it, I don't prep it anymore.
I know what I'm selling.I've watched every piece I recommend fail and then work.
It stays current.When a tool ships a new version, I update my own setup first.
