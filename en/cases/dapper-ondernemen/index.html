CASE STUDY · DAPPER ONDERNEMEN · FINANCIAL COACHING

From 19 separate tools to one platform.

Dapper Ondernemen helps self-employed business owners understand their numbers. The firm itself ran on nineteen tools that didn't talk to each other, and on one person tying it all together. The audit surfaced one diagnosis: not too few tools, too many loose ends. Today the team runs on one platform built for them.

01

Where it started

Dapper Ondernemen is a financial coaching firm: one-on-one programs, workshops and talks for self-employed business owners. A small team with freelancers around it.

The work itself went well. Everything around it didn't. A new client filled in a questionnaire, and then the manual work started: moving data across, writing a kickoff email, chasing missing documents with loose emails. Payments ran through three systems, and nowhere did one place show who had paid when. Content happened when there was time. And every question from the team passed through the same person.

She put it very clearly during our conversation: the executing work had to move away from her as much as possible. That was not why she started her business.

02

The audit

I started by listening and asking. How does a client come in, what happens next, who does what, which tools are there and what do they really do. Then came the report, which we went through together on May 5. She could correct me where I had it wrong. That is part of it.

The report covered four zones: client communication and follow-up, content, team structure, and the firm's own financial admin. Seven priorities, from quick win to strategic. A review of all nineteen tools, with what overlapped and what was barely used. And a proposal for a new division of roles in the team.

But the most important thing was one sentence. Optimising separate tools fixes symptoms. The real problem was fragmentation.

03

The choice: not a twentieth tool

I could have built seven separate improvements. That would have been faster. It would also have meant that a year later there were nineteen things side by side again, just smarter.

We chose the other path: one custom platform where team, clients and numbers come together. Built in phases, in a clear order. We started on May 22.

04

What we built.

One environment behind one login, for the team and for the clients. This is what is in it:

01Team and tasks

One task list for everyone, with the focus of the day, time tracking and notifications. Nobody has to ask what needs doing.

02Leads and clients

Every client has a record with their program and the phase they are in. Intake forms land directly in the platform.

03Automated flows

After a purchase the kickoff email goes out by itself. Then the payment reminder, portal access with a welcome email, and a prep task for the first session.

04Payments and invoices

The payment provider and the accounting software are connected. One screen shows who has paid and who hasn't.

05Daily briefing

Every morning an overview of what is going on today. Since August, without skipping a day.

06Meetings with action items

Recordings of conversations come in automatically, with the action items pulled out. One click sends them to the client or the freelancer.

07Client portal

Clients log in to their own environment. The portal is there and is now being opened up step by step for existing clients.

08Learning platform and shop

Two courses with 48 lessons are ready, next to their own shop. Built, and ready to launch.

05

How we worked together

The project got its own space in my hub, with six phases and a status report every week. A short status meeting every week too. Along the way eighty feedback messages came in, and I am glad about that: every message is something that would otherwise have gone unseen.

The platform itself has a feedback button. Whoever sees something off reports it right where it happens. That keeps tuning an everyday thing, not a separate meeting.

06

What it gives them

I am not going to invent numbers about hours or money here. What is different today:

One place instead of nineteen.The team works in one environment and no longer wonders which tool something was in.
Onboarding a client runs by itself.From purchase to welcome email without anyone retyping anything.
Payments are visible.Who paid is right there. No more laying three systems side by side.
Conversations become actions.What gets decided in a meeting lands as a task with the right person.
The day starts with an overview.The briefing says what needs attention before the inbox opens.
Clients get their own environment.The experience of a program is no longer scattered across emails and loose links.

Honestly: the platform is built wide and is now coming into full use step by step. The more goes in, the more it shows. That is not a weakness in the story, it is how it always goes.
