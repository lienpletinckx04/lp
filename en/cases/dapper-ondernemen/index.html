CASE STUDY · DAPPER ONDERNEMEN · FINANCIAL COACHING

From 19 separate tools to one platform.

Dapper Ondernemen helps solo business owners get a handle on their numbers. The firm itself ran on nineteen tools that didn't talk to each other, with one person holding it all together. The audit came back with a clear diagnosis: they had plenty of tools and far too many loose ends. Today the team works in one platform built for them.

01

Where things stood

Dapper Ondernemen is a financial coaching firm. They offer one-on-one programs, workshops and talks for solo business owners. It's a small team with a few freelancers around it.

The client work was going well. Everything around it was a grind. A new client filled out a questionnaire, and then the manual work began: copying data over, writing a kickoff email, chasing missing documents one email at a time. Payments ran through three systems, and no single place showed who had paid and when. Content got made when there was time. Every question from the team went through the same person.

She said it plainly when we talked: she wanted the day-to-day execution off her plate as much as possible. That wasn't why she started the business.

02

The audit

I started by listening and asking questions. How does a client come in? What happens next? Who does what? Which tools are in use, and what do they really do? Then I wrote the report, and we went through it together on May 5. She corrected me where I'd gotten something wrong, which is part of the process.

The report covered four areas: client communication and follow-up, content, team structure, and the firm's own financial admin. It listed seven priorities, from quick wins to strategic moves. It reviewed all nineteen tools and showed which ones overlapped and which were barely used. It also proposed a new split of roles on the team.

The most important point fit in one sentence. Tuning each tool separately would only treat symptoms, because the real problem was that everything was scattered.

03

The choice: no twentieth tool

I could have built seven separate improvements, and it would have been faster. A year later they'd have had nineteen things sitting side by side again, just smarter ones.

We went the other way and built one custom platform where team, clients and numbers come together. We built it in phases, in a set order, starting on May 22.

04

What we built.

It's one environment behind one login, for the team and for clients. Here's what's in it:

01Team and tasks

One task list for everyone, with a focus for the day, time tracking and notifications. Nobody has to ask what needs to get done.

02Leads and clients

Each client has a record showing their program and what phase they're in. Intake forms come straight into the platform.

03Automated flows

After a purchase, the kickoff email goes out on its own. The payment reminder follows, then portal access with a welcome email, then a prep task for the first session.

04Payments and invoices

The payment provider and the accounting software are connected. One screen shows who has paid and who hasn't.

05Daily briefing

Every morning there's an overview of what's going on that day. It has run every day since August.

06Meetings with action items

Call recordings come in automatically with the action items pulled out. One click sends them to the client or the freelancer.

07Client portal

Clients log in to their own space. The portal is built and is being opened up to existing clients step by step.

08Learning platform and store

Two courses with 48 lessons are ready, along with the firm's own online store. Both are built and ready to launch.

05

How we worked together

The project had its own space in my hub, with six phases and a status report every week. We also had a short status meeting every week. Eighty feedback messages came in along the way, and I'm glad they did. Each one was something we'd have missed otherwise.

The platform has a feedback button built in. If someone spots a problem, they report it right where it happens. That makes fine-tuning part of the daily routine instead of a separate meeting.

06

What they get out of it

They haven't measured hours or money saved, so I won't quote any. Here's what's different today:

One place instead of nineteen.The team works in one environment and no longer has to remember which tool something was in.
Client onboarding runs on its own.From purchase to welcome email, nobody retypes anything.
Payments are visible.You can see who paid. Nobody has to compare three systems.
Conversations turn into action.What gets decided in a meeting lands as a task with the right person.
The day starts with an overview.The briefing shows what needs attention before anyone opens their inbox.
Clients get their own space.The experience of a program is no longer spread across emails and stray links.

The platform covers a lot of ground, and the team is growing into it step by step. The more data goes in, the more it shows them. That's how these projects always go.
