01

Where things stood

Some projects don't start with an audit day. This one started with three things she brought up herself: the schedule, the website and social media.

The schedule was in her head. She knew how often each client needed to come back and which days worked for whom, and every new appointment meant working it all out again. She didn't have a real website. She offered a free video people could sign up for, but after they got it, they never heard from her again. Social media felt like a chore, even though every grooming session produces a before-and-after photo.

Here's what I saw: she already had everything she needed. She had clients, services, followers and a good reputation. The only thing missing was a system around it.

02

The proposal

A full audit day would be overkill for a business this size. I put together a custom proposal in three steps: a booking app with a client system, an email sequence behind her free video, and social media that mostly runs on its own.

She picked the first step and made it bigger: one app that brings her website, her bookings and her clients together. The rest can come later, whenever she's ready.

03

The hardest part: her rules

Buying a booking system is easy. The problem is that no off-the-shelf system knows how a grooming salon works. A small dog takes less time than a big one. You can't book five large dogs back to back. And some clients can only come at certain times.

None of those rules were written down. So we wrote them down. I gave her one document full of questions, and she filled it out herself at her own pace. The booking logic is built on her answers.

04

What we built.

It's one app with a public side and a side for her and her clients:

01The website

Her services, the workshops, the free video and a waitlist for new clients, in her own style with her own photos. It's live.

02The client area

Each client has an account with their dog in it: the grooming schedule, the days that work, and messages with photos.

03Booking by her rules

The system knows how long each dog takes and how many fit in a day. Clients only see time slots that will really work.

04Her own admin

Her schedule, notes on each appointment, a record of no-shows, and all the settings. She changes hours, prices and wording herself.

05Emails that send themselves

A confirmation, a reminder and a follow-up go out without her having to think about it.

06One switch for launch

Online booking is built and turns on with one switch in her settings. Until then, clients see a clean page telling them it's coming soon.

What runs on its own here

There's no AI agent in this one, on purpose. She needed a system that knows her rules. She didn't need an assistant to think for her.

05

How we worked together

We had a short check-in every week and a WhatsApp group for quick questions. I gave her two style directions to choose from, and her feedback was specific: a softer color, different photos, a less traditional font, and the free video front and center.

It's her business, so it has to feel like hers.

06

Where it stands today

Online booking hasn't opened yet, so I have no results to show for it. Here's where things stand:

The website is live.For the first time she has a place of her own that explains what she does and what it costs.
The waitlist works.New clients sign up on the site instead of messaging her over and over.
Her know-how is in a system.The app now knows the rules that only she knew.
Her clients are loaded.Each one has their dog and grooming schedule in the app, ready to book on their own.
She picks the launch date.Online booking opens in January, when the timing works for her.
The app is hers.If we ever stop working together, everything moves to her own accounts.
