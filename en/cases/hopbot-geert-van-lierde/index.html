01

Where things stood

Geert Van Lierde is a beer journalist and author. His website is an archive of years of writing about Belgian beer and beer culture: beer styles, breweries, tasting, and pairing beer with food.

What he had was solid. The trouble was how people look things up now. Someone who wants to know about Trappist beer or a particular beer style types the question into a chatbot. When Geert tried that himself, he got answers that were way off. They read well, they sounded sure of themselves, and they were wrong.

For someone who built a career on getting it right, that's a big deal. Accuracy is what sets him apart.

02

The idea

What if people could ask their question the way they're used to, and the answer came from his work? They'd get a short answer and a link to the full article. His pieces would get found again, and he'd stay the source.

A project like this doesn't need an audit. It needs a good conversation about what the bot is allowed to do and, more importantly, what it isn't.

03

Hopbot's rules

A custom chatbot is only as good as its limits. Hopbot's are strict:

Geert's material comes first. The bot checks his documents and standard answers first, then his own web pages, and only after that does it look further.

It never makes up a link. If the article doesn't exist, there's no link.

It stays in his lane. Belgian beer, beer culture, tasting, brewing and food. For anything else, it politely says that's outside its field.

If it doesn't know, it says so. Then it points you to Geert. The first testers said that was the most human thing about the bot.

It doesn't write articles. That's Geert's job.

04

What we built.

It's a chatbot on his website with its own name and its own face:

01A knowledge base built from his work

His articles, his documents, and a set of standard answers to the questions that come up most.

02A fixed source order

His documents first, then his web pages, and only then anything else. Every answer stays his.

03Short answer, link to the article

The bot summarizes and sends people on. Anyone who wants more reads the full piece on the site.

04Its own character

Hopbot has a face, in a few versions: classic, in the kitchen, and a festive one for the holidays.

05A clean handoff

Where the bot stops, Geert takes over, and his email address is right there.

06Adjustable every month

New articles, new answers, a stricter or looser tone. It keeps growing with his work.

What runs on its own here

This is an AI agent at its simplest: one job, strict rules, nothing extra.

AGENTHopbot itself.

It searches, picks its source in a fixed order, gives a short answer and sends people on, all inside the limits Geert set.

WORKFLOWA handoff when it doesn't know.

It doesn't guess. It sends you to Geert.

05

How we worked together

We had fellow journalists test the bot first. They know the field, so they spotted right away where it went wrong. Their notes became the rules above.

We did a round of tuning in January, and since then we've adjusted it whenever something new comes in.

06

What he gets out of it

First-month numbers: 250 conversations with 161 unique visitors. People asked about beer styles, Trappist beer, how beer is made, and which beer goes with which dish. Here's what matters more:

He stays the source.Anyone who asks a question gets an answer that comes from Geert and leads back to Geert.
His archive is alive again.An article from three years ago gets found by someone asking about it today.
Accuracy beats polish.A bot that admits it doesn't know is worth more than one that guesses.
His name is on it.Hopbot is clearly his. Nobody would mistake it for a generic assistant with a logo on it.
It works while he writes.Questions get answered without him opening his inbox.
It belongs to him.The knowledge, the rules and the tone are all his.

Things quieted down after the first month, as they do once the novelty wears off. That's what we're working on now: a dedicated page for the bot, a more noticeable button, and some attention on his social channels. Building a good chatbot is the first job. Getting people to find it is the second.
