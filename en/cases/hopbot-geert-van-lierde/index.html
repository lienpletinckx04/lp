CASE STUDY · HOPBOT · BEER JOURNALIST GEERT VAN LIERDE

A chatbot that only quotes his own work.

Geert Van Lierde has been writing about Belgian beer for years. His website holds hundreds of articles. And yet people now ask a general AI, which tells them things about beer with great confidence that simply aren't true. So he got his own chatbot. One that only knows what Geert knows.

01

Where it started

Geert Van Lierde is a beer journalist and author. His website is an archive of years of writing about Belgian beer and beer culture: beer styles, breweries, tasting, beer at the table.

The problem wasn't what he had, but how people search. Anyone who wants to know something about Trappist beer or a beer style today types it into a chatbot. And when Geert tested that himself, he saw answers that were completely off. Smoothly written, confident, and wrong.

For someone who has made accuracy his profession, that is not a detail. It is exactly the difference between him and everyone else.

02

The idea

What if the question can be asked the way people are now used to, but the answer comes from his work? A short answer, and a link to the full article. That way his pieces get found again, and he stays the source.

No audit needed for something like this. But a good conversation about what the bot may do, and above all about what it may not.

03

Hopbot's rules

A custom chatbot stands or falls with its boundaries. Hopbot's are sharp:

Geert first, everything else after. The bot looks in his own documents and standard answers first, then on his own web pages, and only then further.

Never an invented link. If the article doesn't exist, there is no link.

Only his territory. Belgian beer, beer culture, tasting, brewing and gastronomy. Outside of that it kindly says this isn't its field.

If it doesn't know, it says so. And it points to Geert himself. The first testers found exactly that the most human thing about the whole bot.

It doesn't write articles. For that you need Geert.

04

What we built.

A chatbot on his website, with its own name and its own face:

01A knowledge base from his own work

His articles, his documents and a set of standard answers to the questions that come back most often.

02A fixed source order

Own documents, then own pages, only then beyond. That keeps every answer his.

03Short answer, link to the article

The bot summarizes and sends people on. Whoever wants more reads the full piece on the site.

04Its own character

Hopbot has a face, in a few versions: classic, in the kitchen, and a festive one for the holidays.

05Honest handover

Where the bot stops, Geert starts. With an email address attached.

06Adjustable every month

New articles, new answers, a stricter or looser tone. It keeps growing with his work.

What runs by itself here

This is an AI agent in its purest form: one job, sharp rules, and nothing else.

AGENTHopbot itself.

It searches, picks its source in a fixed order, answers briefly and sends people on. Within the boundaries Geert set.

WORKFLOWHanding over when it doesn't know.

No guessing, just a clean handover to Geert himself.

05

How we worked together

We first had the bot tested by fellow journalists. People who know the field and so immediately see where it goes wrong. Their remarks became the rules above.

Then a round of tuning in January, and since then we adjust whenever something new comes in.

06

What it gives him

The numbers from the first month: 250 conversations with 161 unique visitors. Questions about beer styles, Trappist, how beer is made, which beer goes with which dish. But the real answer is this:

He stays the source.Whoever asks a question gets an answer that comes from Geert and leads to Geert.
His archive is alive again.An article from three years ago gets found by someone asking about it today.
Right over smooth.A bot that says it doesn't know is worth more than one that guesses.
His name is on it.Hopbot is recognizably his, not a general assistant with a logo on it.
It works while he writes.Questions get answered without him opening his inbox.
It is his.The knowledge, the rules and the tone remain his property.

After the first month things got quieter, as they do once the novelty wears off. That is what we are working on now: a page of its own for the bot, a button that stands out more and attention on his social channels. Building a good chatbot is one thing. Making sure people find it is the second.
