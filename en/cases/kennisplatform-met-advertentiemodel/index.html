01

Where things stood

He has written books in his field, hosts a podcast, gives talks and works as a mediator and expert. His audience is a mix of private individuals and professionals who come to him with very specific questions.

His knowledge was scattered. Some of it was in a book, some in a podcast episode, some in a video, some in case law. To find an answer you had to know where to start, and almost nobody did.

He had a clear idea from our first conversation: put as much as possible on each topic in one place, and add a chatbot that figures out what kind of question you're asking before it sends you to the right spot.

02

No audit, just a clear idea

This project didn't start with an audit day, and it didn't need one. When a client already knows exactly what he wants, he mostly needs someone to build it and think it through with him.

His first request was a chatbot trained on his knowledge and a secure area for his members. The revenue model was part of the plan from day one: sponsors and advertisers would fund the operation, with subscriptions added later. That shaped how we built it, because a platform that has to make money needs a different design than a nice-looking library.

03

From chatbot to platform

The project grew as we went, which often happens once a client sees what's possible. The chatbot turned into a knowledge base. The knowledge base got user roles, with free and paid content. The sponsors got their own login so he doesn't have to place every banner himself.

Halfway through, we moved the platform from a rapid prototyping tool to custom code and its own database, and we did a security and privacy review. You can't see that work from the outside, but it's what separates a prototype from something you can build a business on.

Is your expertise scattered everywhere too?

Books, videos, a podcast, twenty years of experience. That's a platform waiting to be built.

04

What we built.

It's one platform with three sides: one for readers, one for advertisers and one for him.

01The knowledge base

Articles, videos, podcast episodes, publications and case law in one place. Readers can filter by topic, search, save things for later and pick up where they left off.

02The chatbot

It first sorts each question into one of five topic areas. Then it points to the right article, episode or text.

03A chatbot that learns

Questions it couldn't answer go onto a list. That shows him what's still missing, and he fills the gap by uploading a document.

04The advertising platform

Advertisers log in, build their own campaigns and see their own numbers. He approves each one, so nobody emails banners back and forth anymore.

05Payments and invoices

Orders and invoices run through a payment provider with no manual work.

06Publishing on his own

He uploads his own content from a Word file or a PDF and decides what's free and what's for members.

What runs on its own here

An agent out front, workflows behind it, and a platform holding it all up.

AGENTA chatbot that sorts and learns.

It works out what kind of question it's getting, answers from the knowledge base, and keeps a list of what it couldn't answer.

WORKFLOWFrom document to published article.

A Word file or PDF gets processed automatically into an article in the knowledge base.

WORKFLOWApprovals and notifications.

An advertiser's campaign goes to him for approval and then goes live on its own.

PLATFORMKnowledge base and advertising platform.

With roles, payments and numbers for each side.

05

How we worked together

We had a video call about once a month, a shared folder, a feedback document and WhatsApp for quick questions. He added the content himself and sent a screenshot whenever something looked off.

What I liked most about this project is that ideas came from both of us. He knows his field inside and out, and I know what's technically possible. The platform is where those two meet.
