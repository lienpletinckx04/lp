CASE STUDY · KNOWLEDGE PLATFORM · LEGAL EXPERT

Years of expertise, under one roof.

A legal expert in a niche of real estate, with books, a podcast, videos and decades of experience. I am keeping his name to myself for now, his platform is about to go public on its own terms. It all existed, but it was everywhere. It started with a request for a chatbot. It became a full knowledge platform, with its own revenue model built in.

01

Where it started

He wrote books in his field, hosts a podcast, gives talks and acts as a mediator and expert. His audience: private individuals and professionals with very concrete questions.

That knowledge was scattered. Part in a book, part in an episode, part in a video, part in case law. Anyone looking for an answer had to know where to start. And almost nobody does.

His idea was clear from the first conversation: per topic, as much as possible under one roof. And a chatbot that first understands what kind of question it is, and then takes you to the right place.

02

No audit, but a sharp idea

This project did not start with an audit day. It doesn't always have to. Someone who already knows exactly what he wants mostly needs someone to build it and think along.

The first request was a chatbot on his knowledge and a secure environment for his members. The revenue model was there from the start: sponsors and advertisers carrying the operation, later joined by subscriptions. That shaped how we built. A platform that has to earn money is built differently from a nice library.

03

From chatbot to platform

Along the way it grew. That often happens once someone sees what is possible. A chatbot became a knowledge base. The knowledge base got roles, with free and paid content. And the sponsors got their own entrance, so he doesn't have to place every banner himself.

Halfway through we moved the platform from a rapid build environment to its own code and its own database, with a security and privacy review. Less visible, but the difference between a prototype and something you build a business on.

Is your knowledge everywhere too?

Books, videos, a podcast, twenty years of experience. That is a platform that hasn't been built yet.

04

What we built.

One platform with three sides: for the reader, for the advertiser and for himself.

01The knowledge base

Articles, videos, podcast episodes, publications and case law in one place. Filter by theme, search, save for later, and see where you left off.

02The chatbot

It first sorts a question into one of five domains. Then it points to the right article, the right episode or the right text.

03A chatbot that learns

Questions it had no answer to end up in a list. That tells the expert what is still missing, and he fills it with a document.

04The advertising platform

Advertisers log in themselves, build their campaign and see their numbers. He approves. No emailing banners back and forth.

05Payments and invoices

Orders and invoices run through a payment provider, without manual work.

06Publishing himself

He puts his own content up, from a Word file or a PDF, and decides what is free and what is for members.

05

How we worked together

A video call roughly every month, a shared folder, a feedback document and quick questions over WhatsApp. He filled in the content himself and sent a screenshot when something was off.

What I liked most about this project is that the ideas came from both sides. He knows his field down to the details. I know what is technically possible. The platform is the middle of that.

06

What it gives him

I don't publish numbers about members or revenue, and those are his anyway. What is there:

One address for his knowledge.Anyone with a question in his field no longer needs to know which book it was in.
A chatbot that knows his content.Not a general AI, but a guide through his own work.
A revenue model that is built in.Sponsors and advertisers have their own place, with numbers they can see themselves.
He runs it himself.Publishing, approving and adjusting happen without me.
Something to show.A working platform convinces partners faster than a plan on paper.
The platform is his.Code, content and data remain his property.
